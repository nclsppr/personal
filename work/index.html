Leading the Solutions team#

Engineering management · Victor Buck Services

What the team does

We develop and run several of the platforms the company uses to produce and send documents. The engineers know both the products and the code, from services that have been running for years to features we’re building today.

How we set priorities

An incident, a feature a customer is waiting for and an application that needs updating can all need the same people. I work with product, business and operations colleagues to make those choices explicit. Engineers need to know what can wait and why.

Who takes over?

For every major product and technical area, we identify an owner and a backup. I want a colleague to be able to take over with enough context to make a decision, including during an incident.

Day to day, I give engineers the context to choose an implementation themselves. I’m available when a technical decision gets tricky or when we need several teams around the table.

  • Give people regular feedback and gradually hand over more technical and product responsibility
  • Help engineers follow what happens to their products after a technical decision
  • Work directly with architecture, infrastructure, security and production colleagues

What changed

Product knowledge is better shared. Other teams know who to go to, and engineers can make more decisions themselves.

Securing documents and their delivery#

Products and platforms · Victor Buck Services

What the platforms do

Financial institutions and public-sector organizations use these platforms to produce, protect and send confidential documents. That involves managing access to customer portals, secure repositories, high-volume production and several delivery channels.

Modernizing live services

These applications carry years of business rules and customer-specific requirements. Changing their architecture also means understanding how they depend on one another. We have to maintain security, compliance and continuity of service throughout the transition.

My contribution

I help shape the architecture and the modernization plan. With colleagues in the business, development, architecture, infrastructure, security and production teams, we decide what to keep, share or replace.

  • Update the Java applications and move selected services to newer frameworks and architectures
  • Share identity, access management and secure service delivery across products
  • Bring document management, processing and multichannel delivery together
  • Gradually replace ageing components and those that duplicate each other
  • Plan from the outset how we’ll secure, run and maintain each new service

Where things stand

We’ve modernized application foundations and introduced new shared capabilities. Work continues to bring the document and communication services closer together.

A platform for connected homes#

1M+ connected devices · Worldline

What the platform did

At European manufacturers including Gorenje and Bosch/Siemens, it connected household appliances to cloud services and mobile apps.

The problem

More than one million devices could be online at once. Their messages had to travel in real time, and the service had to stay available in several countries.

My contribution

I helped design and develop the platform. I coordinated a team based in Romania and worked with customers in Slovenia, Austria, Germany and Spain.

  • I designed the real-time notification system using MQTT and mobile push notifications.
  • I rebuilt the backend using Spring Cloud microservices.
  • I led the migration to Azure PaaS, Kubernetes and Docker.

In production

The platform sustained that load in production. The new backend made it easier to see what was happening, scale the services and maintain them.

Producing banking documents at scale#

10 million reports a month · Worldline

From data to document

The platform produced PDF, AFP and PostScript documents from databases, XML files and reusable templates in a PCI-DSS-compliant environment.

The problem

Banks need documents to render consistently as volumes rise. The operations team also needs to be able to follow each run and step in if something goes wrong.

My contribution

As Technical Leader, I designed the Java microservices architecture and helped build and deliver the platform.

  • I compared rendering engines for performance, document quality and maintainability.
  • I built an Angular dashboard so the team could monitor and control production.
  • I implemented the CI/CD pipelines and defined performance and reliability indicators.
  • I created an OpenAPI integration model that other Worldline teams went on to use.

In production

Volume reached 10 million banking reports a month.

Engineering principles#

01

Share the context

Engineers make better decisions when they understand the business, operational and technical reasons behind a priority.

02

Know who decides

When it’s clear who will make the decision, colleagues also know who can answer their questions.

03

Stay close to production

I take part in architecture discussions and help resolve incidents. That’s often where we spot things that are making the team’s job harder.

04

Pass on what you know

If only one person knows the command that gets a service running again, it’s time to write it down and show the team.

Technical background#

Most of my work has been on Java platforms and distributed systems in production. Across industrial IoT and document services, I’ve designed APIs and worked with microservices, high-volume processing, event-driven architectures and identity and access management.
